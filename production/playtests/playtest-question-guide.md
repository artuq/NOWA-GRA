# Playtest Question Guide — King of Cringe

> Use this with a fresh tester (someone who has not seen the build before) on the
> current vertical slice: `prototypes/krol-cringeu-vertical-slice/`. Goal: check
> whether each reference game's specific hook is actually landing in our build —
> not just "is it fun" in the abstract.

## Before they play

- Don't explain anything. Hand them the build, say "play through it as if it's a
  game you just downloaded," and watch.
- Pick one mode:
  - **Silent observation** — better for testing *feel* (does the swipe/shake/popup
    register physically). Don't interrupt. Note every moment of hesitation.
  - **Think-aloud** ("narrate what you're doing and why as you go") — better for
    testing *clarity* (does the player understand what to do and why). Gives more
    granular data but slightly changes the experience — pick one, not both.
- Let them complete at least one full cycle: 3 different actions → first card →
  swipe → resolution, unguided.

## Hook questions (ask after they've played)

One combined list — each question is informed by a reference game's specific
hook (Reigns, Melvor Idle, Idle Research: Endless Tycoon, Beggar's Life), but
ask them all as a single set, not split by title:

1. "After you swiped a card, did the reaction text make you want to see what the
   next card would bring?"
2. "Did any card outcome surprise you, or make you want to tell someone about it?"
3. "Did you feel the weight of the choice *before* you swiped, or was it a random
   gesture?"
4. "Did watching the numbers in the HUD go up make you want to do 'just one more'
   action?"
5. "Did you notice the locked action slots? Did they make you curious what
   they'll unlock?"
6. "Did it feel like real progress, or were the numbers just background noise?"
7. "Did it feel like you were building something — a business, a brand — or just
   pressing buttons?"
8. "Did you feel any ownership over what was happening, or did it feel impersonal?"
9. "Did the tone (satire about influencer culture) land as funny or relatable, or
   did it feel flat or preachy?"
10. "Did you want to see how bad or absurd things could get if you kept playing?"

## Closing questions (always ask these, in order)

1. **Loop completion**: "Did you complete the full cycle on your own, without
   guidance?"
2. **Time check**: "How long until you felt like you were actually playing, not
   just clicking around?"
3. **Core fantasy**: "The game is supposed to make you feel in control of your
   own 'content business,' and make you feel the weight of consequences regardless
   of whether they're good or bad for you. Did it? Be specific — not 'kind of.'"
4. **Blockers**: "What stopped you, confused you, or pulled you out of the
   experience?"
5. **The real test**: "Would you want to play again, right now, without me
   asking you to?"
6. **Verdict**: "PROCEED, PIVOT, or KILL — and why, in one sentence."

If any answer is vague, ask: "Can you point to the exact moment that happened?"
Precise observations are the only ones worth recording — vague ones produce a
useless report.
